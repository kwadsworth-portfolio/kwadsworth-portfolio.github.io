Fairness Testing

A genuine disparate-impact finding, not a hypothetical one.

FindingDisparate impact

Age-Band Disparity Detected

Risk: If the model flags younger customers at a materially higher rate than older customers with otherwise similar behaviour, that's indirect age discrimination under the UK Equality Act 2010 and a direct EU AI Act Art. 10 data-governance failure.

Test: Compared churn-flag rate across six age bands on the test set, using the standard four-fifths (80%) rule as a screening threshold.

Result: 18-24 flagged at 53.6% vs 27.6% for 55-64. Disparate impact ratio: 0.516, which fails the 80% threshold.

Mitigation & Residual Risk

What was done about it, and whether it actually worked.

MitigatedPer-group threshold calibration

Before vs After

Decision: Apply per-group decision thresholds so each age band's flag rate converges toward the overall base rate, rather than using one global threshold for every customer.

Result: Disparate impact ratio improved from 0.516 to 0.987, comfortably clearing the 80% rule.

Honest Limitations

What this evidence does and doesn't prove.

Scope
  • Training data is synthetic, generated for this project, not real NovaTel customer data.
  • Per-group threshold calibration fixes the flag-rate disparity but doesn't remove age as a feature the model weights internally; a fuller fix would retrain without it as a direct input.
  • Only age band was tested for fairness here; a production deployment would test additional characteristics and their intersections.
Outcome: A real trained model, a genuine fairness failure detected through testing, and a measured, working mitigation: the full loop that AI governance frameworks describe in the abstract, demonstrated here with actual numbers rather than a hypothetical example.
