1. Overview

Project context — NovaTel UK simulated organisation.

AI GovernanceEU AI ActISO 42001

Project Overview

This project demonstrates how AI governance principles can be applied to a real-world, high-risk AI system rather than treated as an abstract compliance exercise. It extends NovaTel UK — the simulated telecom used elsewhere in this portfolio — with an AI-driven customer churn prediction and retention engine, governed end-to-end across five overlapping frameworks: the EU AI Act, ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, GDPR and PCI DSS.

2. Scope & Objectives

What this project set out to demonstrate.

Scope

Objectives

  • Classify an AI system against EU AI Act risk tiers and justify the classification.
  • Design an AI risk register covering bias, drift, transparency, oversight and third-party data risk.
  • Map governance controls to ISO 42001, NIST AI RMF, GDPR and PCI DSS simultaneously.
  • Define a concrete human-oversight threshold rather than a vague oversight statement.
  • Build a live, filterable dashboard so the register is demonstrably operated, not just documented.

3. AI System & Risk Classification

What the system does and why it's high-risk under the EU AI Act.

System

Customer Churn Prediction & Retention Engine

  • Scores active customers on churn likelihood using usage, billing, tenure and support history.
  • Triggers automated retention offers or flags accounts for human review.
  • Owned by Customer Experience & Data Science, governed by the AI Governance Committee.
EU AI ActHigh-risk

Classification Rationale

  • Falls under Annex III — profiling with material effect on contract terms and access to services.
  • Automated scoring can affect pricing, retention offers and account treatment.
  • Human oversight is mandatory above a defined value threshold (Art. 14).

4. Governance Design

Human oversight, data scope and control boundaries.

ImplementedHuman Oversight

£50/Month Review Threshold

  • Retention offers or contract changes below £50/month are auto-approved.
  • Anything above that threshold, or a full cancellation-risk flag, is hard-blocked pending human sign-off.
  • Satisfies EU AI Act Art. 14 with a concrete, testable control rather than a policy statement.
ImplementedPCI DSS Scope

Payment Data Boundary

  • The model processes tokenised billing metadata only — no raw cardholder data (PAN) ever enters the pipeline.
  • Field-level data flow mapping validates this boundary at ingestion.
  • Keeps the model out of full PCI cardholder-data-environment scope by design, not by assumption.

5. AI Risk Register

Full lifecycle: identification, scoring, response, ownership and monitoring.

8 tracked risksInherent & residual scoring

Live Risk Register

Every risk is tracked with a unique ID, category, likelihood, impact, inherent and residual score, response strategy (Reduce/Avoid/Transfer/Accept), a named owner, a key risk indicator, and a review cadence. Risks are also tagged by nature — Recurring, One-off or Event-triggered — to distinguish ongoing operational risk from risks that close out once a control lands.

6. Challenges & Resolution

Real design decisions made while building this out.

IssueFramework overlap

Conflicting Obligations

GDPR's Art. 22 automated-decision-making rules and the EU AI Act's Art. 14 human-oversight requirement overlap but aren't identical — GDPR focuses on the individual's right to contest, the AI Act on systemic oversight design.

Resolution: the £50/month threshold satisfies both — it guarantees human review before high-impact decisions (AI Act) and gives customers a person to appeal to for any automated outcome (GDPR).

IssueScope creep risk

PCI Scope Ambiguity

Billing data naturally sits near payment data, and it would have been easy to let the model's data inputs quietly expand into full PCI cardholder scope without anyone deciding that deliberately.

Resolution: explicit field-level data flow mapping and a documented scope boundary, tracked as its own risk (AI-005) with tokenisation validation as the control.

7. Framework Alignment

How this project maps across all five frameworks.

EU AI Act / ISO 42001

Governance & Risk Management

  • Art. 9 — risk management system via the live risk register.
  • Art. 10 — data governance via the PII classification scheme.
  • Art. 13/14 — transparency and human oversight via the model card and review threshold.
  • ISO 42001 AIMS clauses mapped to each control above.
NIST AI RMF / GDPR / PCI DSS

Measurement & Data Protection

  • Govern / Map / Measure / Manage functions reflected across the register's response and KRI columns.
  • GDPR Art. 22 addressed via the human-review threshold and explanation rationale.
  • PCI DSS addressed via tokenisation and an explicit no-PAN pipeline boundary.

8. Supporting Artefacts

Real model, real evidence — not staged screenshots.

Genuine model output

A Real Churn Model, Not a Mock-Up

Rather than describing bias testing in the abstract, this project trains an actual logistic regression churn model on a synthetic NovaTel dataset, then runs a genuine fairness check across age bands — a protected characteristic under the UK Equality Act 2010. The model really did initially fail the 80% disparate-impact rule (ratio 0.516), and the mitigation applied really did fix it (ratio 0.987). Every chart and figure in the evidence pack is generated directly from this model's output.
